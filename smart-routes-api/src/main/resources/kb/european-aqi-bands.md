# The European Air Quality Index (European AQI)

ClearWay rates air with the European Air Quality Index, published by the European Environment Agency and provided to ClearWay through Open-Meteo. The index turns readings of several pollutants into one number and one band, so people can see at a glance how clean the air is.

## How the index is worked out

The index looks at fine particles (PM2.5), coarse particles (PM10), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), ozone (O₃) and sulphur dioxide (SO₂). Each pollutant is placed in a band on its own, and the overall index is set by the worst one. So one high pollutant is enough to make the whole reading poor, even if the others are low.

## The six bands

On the 0 to 100+ scale that Open-Meteo reports, the bands are:

- **Good (0–20):** the air is clean. Enjoy your usual outdoor activities.
- **Fair (20–40):** still fine for almost everyone.
- **Moderate (40–60):** people who are sensitive may want to cut down on long or hard outdoor exercise.
- **Poor (60–80):** sensitive people should reduce hard outdoor activity. Others may notice irritation.
- **Very poor (80–100):** everyone should consider reducing hard outdoor activity; sensitive people should avoid it.
- **Extremely poor (above 100):** avoid hard outdoor activity; sensitive people should stay indoors where possible.

## How ClearWay uses the bands

ClearWay colours each route by its band. Its poor-air alert for saved routes is sent when a route's score goes above 75, in the upper half of the Poor band. A lower index number always means cleaner air.
