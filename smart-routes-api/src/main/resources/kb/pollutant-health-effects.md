# Health effects of air pollutants

## Fine particles (PM2.5)

PM2.5 particles are small enough to travel deep into the lungs, and the smallest reach the bloodstream. Short spells of high PM2.5 can trigger coughing, breathlessness and asthma attacks. Breathing it over years raises the risk of heart disease, stroke, lung cancer and chronic lung disease. Of all common pollutants, PM2.5 is linked to the most harm to health.

## Coarse particles (PM10)

PM10 mostly settles in the nose, throat and upper airways. It irritates the eyes and throat and can make asthma and bronchitis worse.

## Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂)

NO₂ inflames the lining of the airways. It makes people with asthma more sensitive to triggers such as pollen, and long exposure in childhood is linked to developing asthma. Because it comes from traffic, people walking or cycling right beside busy roads breathe the most.

## Ozone (O₃)

Ozone irritates the airways and can cause chest tightness, coughing and pain on breathing in deeply. It reduces how well the lungs work, especially during exercise, when people breathe faster and deeper.

## Carbon monoxide (CO)

Outdoors, CO is rarely high enough to cause harm, but near heavy traffic it reduces how much oxygen the blood can carry, which matters most for people with heart disease.

## Who is most sensitive

Some groups feel the effects sooner and more strongly:

- children, whose lungs are still growing and who breathe faster;
- older adults;
- people with asthma, COPD or other lung conditions;
- people with heart disease or diabetes;
- pregnant people;
- anyone exercising hard outdoors, because they breathe in far more air.

People in these groups should act on Moderate or Poor air sooner than others. Anyone with symptoms that worsen should follow their own care plan and talk to a doctor; this guidance is general information, not medical advice.
