# Exercising and commuting when the air is poor

Being active is good for health, and for most people the benefits of walking, running or cycling are greater than the harm from city air. On bad air days, a few simple changes cut how much pollution you breathe without giving up exercise.

## Choose the time

- Traffic pollution such as NO₂ is usually worst in the morning and evening rush hours. Mid-morning or early afternoon is often cleaner.
- Ozone is the opposite: it builds up on sunny afternoons, so on hot days exercise early in the morning.
- Check the forecast first. ClearWay's best-time strip shows the next 24 hours so you can pick a cleaner slot.

## Choose the route

- Pollution drops sharply even one or two streets away from a busy road. Back streets, parks and riversides are usually cleaner.
- Avoid waiting at junctions with idling traffic, and keep a little distance from the kerb where you can.
- ClearWay compares routes by the pollution along them; a route that is a few minutes longer is often much cleaner.

## Adjust the effort

- When the air is Moderate or worse, swap a hard session for an easier one, or shorten it. Breathing hard pulls far more air, and pollution, into the lungs.
- When the air is Very poor or worse, consider exercising indoors.
- People in sensitive groups, such as those with asthma, should follow their care plan and keep any reliever inhaler with them.

## On the commute

- Inside a car, the air can be as polluted as outside, sometimes more, because the car sits in the traffic fumes. Keep windows shut and the air on recirculate in heavy traffic.
- Walking or cycling on quieter streets often means breathing less pollution than driving on main roads.

Masks only help if they are well-fitting respirators such as FFP2 or N95; loose cloth or surgical masks do little against fine particles.
